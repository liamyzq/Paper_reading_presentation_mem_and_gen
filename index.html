If training learns the empirical score, why do diffusion models generate anything new?

A

Dynamical Regimes

B

Selective Underfitting

A real neural network is not exact everywhere. Where does it fit the empirical score, and where does it deviate?

\(x_t\) noisy state at diffusion time \(t\) \(p_t^e\) noised empirical density \(s_\star\) empirical score \(s_\theta\) learned score
02 · Notation bridge

One notation for both papers

\[ x_t=a_t x_0+b_t\epsilon,\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d) \]
\[ p_t^e(x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal N\!\left(x; a_t x_0^{(i)}, b_t^2 I_d\right), \qquad s_\star(x,t)=\nabla_x\log p_t^e(x) \]

Data and scale

  • \(x_0^{(i)}\): training sample \(i\)
  • \(n\): number of training samples
  • \(d\): data dimension

Forward schedule

  • \(a_t\): retained signal
  • \(b_t\): noise scale
  • Dynamical Regimes: \(a_t=e^{-t}\), \(b_t^2=\Delta_t=1-e^{-2t}\)

Population vs. empirical

  • \(p_t\): noised population density
  • \(p_t^e\): noised finite-data density
  • \(\rho=\log n/d\): exponential data-rate parameter

Time conventions differ across the papers. Throughout the talk, “early generation” means high noise and “late generation” means low noise.

03 · Exact empirical-score dynamics

The exact reverse process must end at the empirical distribution

\[ p_0^e(x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta(x-x_0^{(i)}) \]
  • Forward diffusion maps \(p_0^e\) to \(p_t^e\).
  • The reverse SDE using \(s_\star=\nabla\log p_t^e\) time-reverses this path.
  • At \(t=0\), the endpoint distribution is again \(p_0^e\): a training point.
The question is not whether exact dynamics memorizes, but when sample-specific memorization becomes visible.

Figure: Biroli et al., “Dynamical Regimes of Diffusion Models,” Fig. 1.

04 · Main phenomenon

Noise → class commitment → training-point commitment

Regime I

Noise-like bundle. The future class is still undecided.

→

Regime II

A semantic class is selected, but no individual training point is selected.

→

Regime III

The trajectory enters the attraction basin of one training point.

\[t_S:\ \text{speciation time}\]
and
\[t_C:\ \text{collapse time}\]
The two transitions have different causes: spectral signal recovery versus sample-level density condensation.
05 · Speciation

A dominant covariance mode becomes distinguishable from noise

\[ C(t)=a_t^2 C_0+b_t^2 I_d \]
\[ \Lambda e^{-2t_S}\approx 1 \]
\(C_0\) clean-data covariance \(C(t)\) noisy covariance \(\Lambda\) largest eigenvalue of \(C_0\)

Signal–noise balance

  • Along the strongest data direction, structured variance is \(\Lambda e^{-2t}\).
  • Isotropic noise contributes approximately one at high noise.
  • Reverse trajectories can separate into classes once the dominant signal becomes visible.
Speciation recovers a macroscopic data direction, not an individual sample.
06 · Speciation proof route I

Landau expansion: speciation is a curvature instability

  1. Expand \(\log p_t(x)\) in the small parameter \(e^{-t}\) at high noise.
  2. The quadratic term is controlled by \(M(t)\approx I_d-e^{-2t}C_0\).
  3. The curvature first becomes unstable in the top-eigenvector direction of \(C_0\).
  4. This creates a symmetry-breaking split in the reverse dynamics.
\[ \log p_t(x)\approx K(t)+\ell(t)^\top x-\frac{1}{2b_t^2}x^\top M(t)x+\cdots \]
\[ M(t)\approx I_d-e^{-2t}C_0 \]
\(K(t)\) term independent of \(x\) \(\ell(t)\) linear coefficient \(M(t)\) quadratic curvature matrix
07 · Speciation proof route II

Gaussian mixture reduces the class decision to one order parameter

\[ p_0=\tfrac12\mathcal N(m,\sigma^2I_d)+\tfrac12\mathcal N(-m,\sigma^2I_d), \qquad \|m\|^2=d\widetilde\mu^2 \]
\[ q(t)=\frac{m^\top x_t}{\sqrt d} \]
  • \(m\): vector separating the two class centers.
  • \(\sigma^2\): within-class variance.
  • \(\widetilde\mu\): class separation per dimension.
  • The effective potential for \(q(t)\) changes from symmetric noise-like behavior to two class-selective branches.

Figure: Biroli et al., Gaussian-mixture speciation experiment.

08 · Operational diagnostic

Clone a trajectory and ask whether both futures choose the same class

\[ \phi(t)=\Pr\!\left(\text{two clones created at }t\text{ end in the same class}\right) \]
  • Before speciation: independent future noise can change the class, so \(\phi(t)\approx\tfrac12\).
  • After speciation: the class is already fixed, so \(\phi(t)\approx1\).
  • For the Gaussian mixture, all directions orthogonal to \(m\) integrate out, leaving a one-dimensional formula.
Rescaling time by \(t_S=\tfrac12\log\Lambda\) aligns the transition across very different image datasets.

Figure: Biroli et al., clone-based speciation on MNIST, CIFAR, ImageNet and LSUN.

09 · Why Regime II generalizes

After the class is chosen, the score becomes a population-class score

\[ s_i(x,t)=-\frac{x_i}{\Gamma_t}+m_i\frac{e^{-t}}{\Gamma_t} \tanh\!\left(\frac{e^{-t}m^\top x}{\Gamma_t}\right), \qquad \Gamma_t=b_t^2+\sigma^2e^{-2t} \]
\[ m^\top x\to +\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad s_i(x,t)\to -\frac{x_i}{\Gamma_t}+m_i\frac{e^{-t}}{\Gamma_t} \]

Before speciation

The sign of \(m^\top x\) is unresolved.

After speciation

The \(\tanh\) saturates to \(+1\) or \(-1\).

Interpretation

The remaining reverse process is that of one full Gaussian class, not one training point.

This is intermediate-time generalization: exact empirical dynamics is class-level now, but will still collapse later.
10 · Collapse

One training point competes against the aggregate of all others

\[ x=a_1e^{-t}+\sqrt{\Delta_t}\,z,\qquad z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d) \]
\[ p_t^e(x)\propto Z_1+Z_{2:n} \]
  • \(a_1\): the training point from which \(x\) was noised.
  • \(Z_1\): density contribution from \(a_1\).
  • \(Z_{2:n}\): total contribution from the other \(n-1\) samples.
  • If \(Z_1\) dominates, the score points back to \(a_1\).

Figure: Biroli et al., Gaussian-mixture collapse.

11 · Collapse proof procedure

The other samples form a Random Energy Model partition function

\[ Z_{2:n}=\sum_{\mu=2}^{n}e^{-E_\mu}, \qquad E_\mu=\frac{\|x-a_\mu e^{-t}\|^2}{2\Delta_t} \]
\[ \psi(t)=\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac1d\log Z_{2:n} \]
\(E_\mu\) distance-based random energy \(\psi(t)\) aggregate exponential rate
  1. Each energy is order \(d\), while \(n=e^{\rho d}\).
  2. A large-deviation rate function counts how many samples attain each energy density.
  3. Laplace optimization yields the leading exponent \(\psi(t)\).
  4. The self contribution satisfies \(d^{-1}\log Z_1\to-\tfrac12\).
\[ \psi(t_C)=-\frac12 \]
Collapse is a condensation transition: the density changes from many-sample support to one-sample dominance.
12 · Curse of dimensionality

Delaying sample-specific collapse requires exponentially many data

\[ t_C=\frac12\log\!\left(1+\frac{\sigma^2}{n^{2/d}-1}\right) \]
\[ \rho=\frac{\log n}{d},\qquad n=e^{\rho d} \]

Polynomial \(n\)

Then \(\rho\to0\), and the training set poorly covers the high-dimensional population.

Exponential \(n\)

Finite \(\rho\) can keep the non-collapsed regime visible down to order-one noise levels.

Exact endpoint

For any finite dataset, exact time reversal still ends at the empirical distribution at \(t=0\).

13 · General collapse diagnostic

Typical-set volume turns the REM result into an entropy criterion

\[ h(t)=-\frac1d\int p_t(x)\log p_t(x)\,dx \]
\[ h^{\mathrm{sep}}(t)=\frac{\log n}{d}+\frac12+\frac12\log(2\pi b_t^2) \]
\[ h(t_C)=h^{\mathrm{sep}}(t_C) \]
  • \(h(t)\): entropy per dimension of the population noised density.
  • \(h^{\mathrm{sep}}(t)\): entropy per dimension of \(n\) separated Gaussian lumps.
  • The zero of the excess entropy estimates when empirical lumps can cover the population typical set.

Figure: Biroli et al., collapse diagnostics on ImageNet and LSUN.

14 · The missing mechanism

Exact empirical-score dynamics explains collapse—but practical models often escape it

Exact everywhere

\[s_\theta=s_\star\]

The trajectory follows \(p_t^e\) and eventually returns to a training point.

→

Real neural score

\[s_\theta\neq s_\star\ \text{somewhere}\]

The decisive question is where this deviation occurs in state space.

Selective Underfitting changes the axis of analysis: from when the dynamics changes to where the model is supervised.
15 · Selective Underfitting

Training mass concentrates on shells around individual data points

\[ \mathcal T_t(\delta)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\left\{x:\left|\|x-a_t x_0^{(i)}\|-b_t\sqrt d\right|\le w_t(\delta)\right\} \]
  • \(\mathcal T_t(\delta)\): supervision region containing at least \(1-\delta\) of training inputs.
  • \(w_t(\delta)\): shell-width bound from Gaussian concentration.
  • When shells barely overlap, one softmax weight dominates.
\[ s_\star(x,t)\approx-\frac{x-a_t x_0^{(i)}}{b_t^2} \]

Inside the \(i\)-th shell, the empirical score mainly points to training sample \(x_0^{(i)}\).

Figure: Song et al., selective-underfitting schematic.

16 · Off-shell inference

Free generation leaves the high-probability training region early

\[ r^{(i)}(x,t)=\frac{\|x-a_t x_0^{(i)}\|}{b_t\sqrt d}, \qquad r_\star= r^{(i_\star)},\quad i_\star=\arg\min_i|r^{(i)}-1| \]
  • \(r_\star\approx1\): the point lies near at least one supervision shell.
  • Training inputs remain concentrated near one.
  • Inference trajectories rapidly move to \(r_\star>1\).
The model is queried where the empirical score received almost no direct probability-weighted supervision.

Figure: Song et al., training versus inference distance from the supervision region.

17 · Contrastive scaling

Larger models fit the empirical score on-shell—but deviate more off-shell

\[ s_\theta(x,t)\approx \begin{cases} s_\star(x,t),&x\in\mathcal T_t(\delta),\\ \text{learned extrapolation},&x\notin\mathcal T_t(\delta). \end{cases} \]
  • Underfitting is measured by \(\|s_\theta-s_\star\|^2\).
  • Across SiT model scales, supervision-region error decreases.
  • Extrapolation-region deviation from the empirical score increases.
This is not global underfitting. It is region-selective fitting and deviation.

Figure: Song et al., quantitative evidence for selective underfitting.

18 · Same model, different starting region

Starting from a noised training image reveals memorization inside the supervision region

On-shell initialization

A noised training image is denoised back to its original training sample over a broad low-noise range.

Free initialization

A Gaussian-noise trajectory moves off-shell and follows a different learned vector field.

Figure: Song et al., memorization phase transition from noisy training examples.

19 · Controlled intervention

Broaden the supervision region while fixing the target score—and generalization degrades

\[ \mathcal D_{\mathrm{score}}\ \text{fixed},\qquad \mathcal D_{\mathrm{region}}\ \text{varied},\qquad \mathcal D_{\mathrm{score}}\subseteq\mathcal D_{\mathrm{region}} \]
\(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{score}}\) samples defining the empirical target score \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{region}}\) samples defining where training inputs are drawn

Figure: Song et al., freedom-of-extrapolation intervention.

20 · Synthesis

Preserve coarse structure; avoid sample-specific attraction

1

Spectral structure

Large population-level directions become visible at speciation.

2

Exact-score collapse

At low noise, a single empirical component can dominate the density.

3

Learned escape

Practical trajectories move off-shell, where the score is shaped by extrapolation rather than direct empirical supervision.

A successful model can retain enough coarse score structure to speciate, while deviating before sample-level empirical attraction forces collapse.
21 · Takeaways

What the two papers explain together

A

Exact score is not enough

It gives a useful middle regime, but its exact endpoint is memorization.

B

Two transitions, two mechanisms

Speciation is covariance-driven; collapse is entropy- and coverage-driven.

C

Generalization is off-shell

Novel generation depends on how architecture and training shape the score outside the supervision region.

\[ \text{Open problem: predict and control }s_\theta(x,t)\text{ for }x\notin\mathcal T_t(\delta). \]

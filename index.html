Memorization and Generalization in Diffusion Models

A spectral view of why useful structure can emerge early in training, while exact training-sample recovery appears later.

NTK / lazy-training lens Spectral dynamics Diffusion-time dependence Liam Ye
01 · Central question

How can one model generalize first and memorize later?

A

Early training

Generated samples capture broad, shared structure rather than reproducing individual examples.

B

Transition

More spectral directions become fitted, increasing detail but also sensitivity to the finite dataset.

C

Late training

Small-scale, sample-specific modes can eventually reconstruct or closely track training points.

Inline-size test: the score network \(s_\theta(x_t,t)\) should sit on the same baseline and at the same visual size as the surrounding sentence.
02 · Training objective

Score matching couples optimization time and diffusion time

\[ \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{E}_{t\sim p(t)} \mathbb{E}_{x_t\sim q_t(\cdot\mid x_i)} \!\left[ \lambda(t) \left\|s_\theta(x_t,t)-\nabla_{x_t}\log q_t(x_t\mid x_i)\right\|_2^2 \right]. \]

Optimization time \(\tau\)

Controls how far training has progressed and which kernel eigenmodes have been learned.

Diffusion time \(t\)

Controls the noise level, target score geometry, and potentially the spectrum of the relevant Gram matrix.

03 · Spectral mechanism

Large-eigenvalue modes are learned faster

shared / smooth modessample-specific modes

Under linearized gradient flow, each eigendirection evolves independently:

\[ a_j(\tau) = \bigl(1-e^{-\eta\lambda_j\tau}\bigr)y_j + e^{-\eta\lambda_j\tau}a_j(0). \]
  • Large \(\lambda_j\): rapid fitting.
  • Small \(\lambda_j\): delayed fitting.
  • A separated spectrum naturally creates distinct training regimes.
04 · Training regimes

A simple three-stage picture

Underfit

Neither spectral bulk is sufficiently learned.

→

Generalizing window

Dominant modes are fitted while weak modes remain suppressed.

→

Memorizing regime

Weak, data-specific modes become increasingly resolved.

\[ \underbrace{\tau \ll (\eta\lambda_{\mathrm{large}})^{-1}}_{\text{underfit}} \quad\longrightarrow\quad \underbrace{(\eta\lambda_{\mathrm{large}})^{-1}\!\ll\tau\ll(\eta\lambda_{\mathrm{small}})^{-1}}_{\text{selective fitting}} \quad\longrightarrow\quad \underbrace{\tau \gg (\eta\lambda_{\mathrm{small}})^{-1}}_{\text{memorization}}. \]
05 · Important open check

Does the mechanism hold uniformly across diffusion time \(t\)?

What may vary with \(t\)?

  • The scale and geometry of noisy inputs \(x_t\).
  • The target score magnitude and smoothness.
  • The NTK Gram spectrum \(K_t\), including bulk separation.
  • The effective weighting induced by \(p(t)\lambda(t)\).

A direct diagnostic

\[ K_t = U_t\,\mathrm{diag}\!\bigl(\lambda_1(t),\ldots,\lambda_n(t)\bigr)U_t^\top. \]

Track the spectrum, bulk gap, and modal fitting rates across representative noise levels—not only at one selected \(t\).

The strongest empirical validation would show that the proposed spectral separation persists over the practically important portion of the noise schedule.
06 · Takeaways

What this viewpoint explains—and what it still needs

1

Mechanism

Spectral bias gives a clean account of why structure can be learned before sample-specific detail.

2

Prediction

A useful early-stopping window should align with the separation between fast and slow eigendirections.

3

Boundary

The explanation is most convincing when its spectrum and time-scale claims are verified across \(t\) and beyond the lazy-training approximation.
